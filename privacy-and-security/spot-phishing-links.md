# Spot Phishing Links and Messages

**Purpose:** Recognize scam emails and texts before you click.
**Requirements:** None.

## Warning signs

- The sender's real address does not match the name shown.
- The message creates urgency, fear, or a prize you did not enter for.
- The link text says one thing and the address says another. Hover over a link (or long-press on a phone) to see where it goes.
- The domain is slightly wrong, such as `paypa1.com` or an extra word before the real name.
- It asks for passwords, codes, or payment by gift card.

## Safer habits

1. Do not use links in the message. Open the site yourself or use its official app.
2. Do not open attachments you did not expect.
3. Check a suspicious link with a public scanner such as VirusTotal or urlscan.io.
4. HTTPS and a padlock do not prove a site is safe.

## If you clicked

1. Change the affected password right away and turn on two-factor authentication.
2. Run a security scan on the device.
3. Contact your bank if you entered card or account details.
4. Report phishing to your email provider, or forward it to `reportphishing@apwg.org`.
